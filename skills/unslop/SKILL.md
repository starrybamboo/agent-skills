# Unslop

Make prose sound like a person wrote it. Preserve the writer's meaning, facts, point of view, and intended tone. Do not invent specifics, sources, opinions, or measurements to make a rewrite feel concrete.

## Process

1. Identify the patterns below in the source or draft.
2. Rewrite only the passages that need it. Keep deliberate voice and quirks.
3. Restore human texture where the material supports it.
4. Read the result once more and ask, "What still makes this sound generated?" Fix the remaining tells.

The work is complete when the prose is specific, natural at sentence level, faithful to the source, and free of the applicable patterns below.

## Human texture

- Keep or sharpen opinions already present instead of flattening them into neutral pros and cons.
- Vary sentence length and rhythm. Let short sentences sit beside longer ones.
- Preserve real tension and mixed reactions when the material contains them.
- Use first person when it fits the speaker and context.
- Prefer natural flow over mechanically perfect symmetry.
- Replace generic reactions with concrete details already supported by the source.

## Content

- **Puffery.** Replace phrases such as "pivotal moment," "testament to," "evolving landscape," "setting the stage for," "indelible mark," and "deeply rooted" with what happened.
- **Name-dropping.** Do not list media outlets as borrowed authority. Name the relevant source and say what it reported.
- **Superficial participles.** Delete dangling phrases built around "highlighting," "ensuring," "reflecting," "showcasing," or "fostering," or replace them with a supported claim.
- **Promotion.** Replace words such as "nestled," "vibrant," "breathtaking," "groundbreaking," "renowned," "stunning," and "must-visit" with neutral descriptions unless promotional copy is the requested tone.
- **Vague attribution.** Name the source behind "experts believe," "industry reports suggest," or "some critics argue." Remove the claim if no source exists.
- **Formulaic resilience.** Replace "despite challenges, it continues to thrive" and similar framing with the relevant facts.

## Language

- **Stock AI vocabulary.** Replace words such as "additionally," "crucial," "delve," "enduring," "enhance," "fostering," "garner," "interplay," "intricate," "pivotal," "showcase," "tapestry," "testament," "underscore," and abstract uses of "landscape" or "vibrant" with plain, precise words.
- **Fancy forms of "is" or "has."** Replace "serves as," "stands as," "boasts," and "features" when "is" or "has" says the same thing.
- **Contrast templates.** State the point directly instead of using "not just X, but Y."
- **Forced threes.** Use the number of ideas the material actually contains.
- **Synonym cycling.** Pick the clearest term and repeat it when it names the same thing.
- **False ranges.** Use "from X to Y" only for a real scale or progression. Otherwise list the topics.

## Style

- **Dashes.** Avoid em dashes. Use a period or comma. Do not replace them with parentheses, en dashes, or hyphens used as dashes.
- **Colons.** Use colons for lists or examples, not as a routine mid-sentence connector.
- **Boldface.** Reserve bold for real hierarchy or emphasis. Do not bold every proper noun or acronym.
- **Inline-header lists.** Replace labels that merely repeat the following sentence with prose. A short label is useful only when the following text adds new information.
- **Headings.** Use sentence case.
- **Emojis.** Remove decorative emojis from headings and bullets.
- **Quotation marks.** Use straight quotes unless the requested style requires typographic quotes.

## Communication artifacts

- Remove chatbot filler such as "I hope this helps," "Let me know if," "Of course," and "Certainly."
- Replace cutoff disclaimers such as "while specific details are limited" with sourced facts, or remove them.
- Respond directly instead of opening with praise or agreement such as "Great question" or "You're absolutely right."

## Filler

- Shorten filler: "in order to" becomes "to" and "due to the fact that" becomes "because." Delete "it is important to note that."
- Reduce stacked hedges to the one qualifier the evidence warrants.
- Replace generic conclusions such as "the future looks bright" with a specific fact or plan.

## Jargon

Prefer the concrete term over abstract metaphor nouns such as "substrate," "wedge," "vector," "locus," "vantage," "nexus," "primitive," metaphorical "harness," "surface," "bedrock," "scaffolding," "modality," "paradigm," "gold-plating," "ratchet," "evacuate," "endgame," "north star," and "flywheel." Name the actual base, method, location, operation, limit, or phase.

## Plain speech

- **State the mechanism.** Replace phrases about how something feels with a concrete instruction, fact, behavior, or number. If a sentence could appear unchanged in another project's documentation, make it specific to this project or cut it.
- **One idea per sentence.** Split a sentence when readers must backtrack to parse it. Drop clauses that do not earn their space.
- **Active voice.** Name the actor when it matters. Passive voice is fine when the actor is unknown or irrelevant.
- **Strong verbs.** Remove an adverb that props up a weak verb. Use a measured value when one exists.
- **Plain words.** Prefer "use," "help," "many," and "if" over "utilize," "leverage," "facilitate," "numerous," and "in the event that."
